# Stage 10 — Notifications and advanced reports

Stage 09 payment integration is deferred; see [the reminder list](reminder.md#stage-09--payment-gateway-and-top-ups-deferred). Reports use existing campaign events and Stage 08 ledger entries. They do not verify gateway payments.

## Notifications

The client bell shows unread account and personal notices, refreshed every minute while the page is visible. Each user has independent read status. Audio decisions, terminal campaign states and recent failed/uncertain attempts generate account notices. Low available credit and billing review conditions generate personal alerts once per episode; recovery rearms the alert. A user sets their low-credit threshold in the wallet currency; zero disables it.

Email and SMS require a verified destination and an explicit personal opt-in. They also require `VOICE_ALERT_DELIVERY_ENABLED=true`, working Laravel mail/TXTConnect configuration and a queue worker. The switch defaults to false. Enabling a preference does not send historical notices. Messages contain only a generic instruction to sign in; private report data, campaign details and credentials are excluded.

Delivery history is visible to the recipient. Retries stop after three attempts; the per-user hourly budget is ten attempts, including failures. Preferences, verification, suspension and account access are checked again before sending. Interrupted deliveries become unknown instead of being blindly retried. Transport failures can still be ambiguous, so providers without idempotency may deliver a duplicate during a retry.

## Reports and definitions

Clients open `/client/reports`; authorized internal staff open `/reports`. Campaigns, attempts, IVR responses and billing are separate datasets. Client queries remain account-scoped. Internal analysts require `reports.view`; billing requires `billing.view`. Finance-only users can access billing reports. Client and campaign-owner filters are available to internal users.

Dates are inclusive calendar days in the selected timezone, translated into an exclusive next-day UTC boundary. The maximum range is 366 days. Campaign reports filter campaign creation time; attempts filter attempt creation time; IVR filters response time; billing filters ledger posting time. A campaign filter does not change those date definitions.

Campaign funnels count frozen execution recipients and unique answered/delivered recipients, so retries do not inflate those recipient counts. Attempt reports count individual attempts and expose answer/playback evidence separately. Answer and playback rates use paired denominators. An answer or playback event alone does not establish that the recipient heard the recording. IVR reports group recorded node/digit responses. Billing reports use posted immutable ledger entries and group amounts by currency and precision; currencies are never added together.

## Private exports and schedules

CSV and genuine XLSX exports are generated by queued jobs, capped at 5,000 rows and retained for seven days. Oversized exports fail rather than silently truncating; narrow the filters. CSV cells guard against spreadsheet formulas; XLSX strings use explicit text cells. Money retains exact decimal strings. Exports exclude private billing evidence, media tokens and connection credentials.

Each file belongs to its requesting user. Access, account status and permissions are rechecked during generation and download; checksums detect changed files. Artifacts stay on private storage. Users can request at most ten exports per hour and keep five active schedules. Export provenance cannot be edited after creation.

Daily and weekly schedules use a chosen timezone/local time and the previous 7, 30 or 90 complete days. A due slot creates one export; repeated maintenance does not duplicate it. Missed slots do not trigger a backfill flood. Revoked access pauses schedules. Users can stop their own schedules. Client completion notices link to authenticated downloads, and optional email/SMS sends a generic notification. Staff retrieve their scheduled exports from their private report history. Files are not sent as attachments.

## Deployment and operation

Apply migrations, deploy the report CSS/bell JavaScript assets, refresh application caches and restart normal queue workers. No new role grants are required.

```sh
php artisan migrate --force
php artisan queue:restart
```

Keep the Laravel scheduler running every minute. It invokes `client:sync-notifications` and `reports:maintain`, each protected against overlap. These commands can also be run manually for maintenance. Queue workers execute `GenerateReportExport` and, when enabled, `DeliverClientAlert`. Retention maintenance deletes expired files and cleans stale failed generation artifacts.

External alert delivery remains disabled until the operator configures and enables it. This stage does not enable campaign dispatch, fund wallets or activate payment collection. Production deployment still requires its own migration, worker restart and controlled channel acceptance.

## Operational log files

Daily rotating files in `storage/logs` separate `email-YYYY-MM-DD.log`, `sms-YYYY-MM-DD.log`, `broadcast-YYYY-MM-DD.log` and `reports-YYYY-MM-DD.log`. Retention uses `LOG_DAILY_DAYS` (14 by default). General exceptions continue in `laravel.log`. Email/SMS transport logs contain correlation IDs, the final four destination characters and sanitized status metadata, without message bodies, credentials or raw provider responses. An accepted SMS is not proof of handset receipt. The development mail `log` transport defaults to the email channel and deliberately writes the full development email; protect these files and use a real transport for production. Existing explicit `MAIL_LOG_CHANNEL` configuration takes precedence.
